# Drug Interaction Review Protocol

1. List every home medication and check each pair for a clinically significant interaction —
   do not limit yourself to the drug being ordered; home-medication-to-home-medication
   interactions matter too (e.g. warfarin + amiodarone).
2. For each interaction found, state the mechanism only if it is general pharmacology
   knowledge (e.g. "amiodarone inhibits CYP2C9 metabolism of warfarin"); do not attribute a
   general drug-drug interaction to the ordered drug's own label unless the label actually
   says so.
3. Check lab values for signs an interaction may already be manifesting (e.g. supratherapeutic
   INR with a warfarin+amiodarone combination) and flag it as an active safety concern, not
   just a theoretical risk.
4. Recommend a specific action (hold, dose-adjust, increase monitoring frequency) rather than
   a vague "monitor closely."
